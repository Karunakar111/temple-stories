# Gudi Kathalu — 30-episode launch slate and Episode 2 brief

Prepared 23 November 2026. Scope: Telugu-language stories from Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and Kerala. Status: editorial plan and source leads, not 30 approved scripts.

## Editorial decision

Start with Komuravelli Mallanna as slot 1 and Kolanupaka Someshwara as slot 2. If EP01 is already published, count it as the first release. The slate contains 14 Telangana episodes, 14 Andhra Pradesh episodes and two national specials. Puri closes the first seven episodes at EP07. Padmanabhaswamy follows at EP14. Warangal Bhadrakali moves to EP09 and Ahobilam to EP29. Keep Inavolu and Vontimitta for the next batch.

The main audience promise is: a temple people recognise, a story or practice they have not understood, and an honest account of what the sources actually say. This is an editorial hypothesis to test, not a promise of virality.

Keep each episode complete and let its evidence-backed story determine duration. A temple may support an origin story, a ritual explanation, a biography or a heritage narrative. It does not need an invented adventure to fit the channel.

## How to use the slate

The angles below are research assignments, not verified statements for narration. A source lead proves only what that source actually says. An official tourism page is not proof of a miracle or an independently established construction date. No paid job is authorised by this document.

Do not announce an inflexible 30-day public challenge yet. First hold three finished episodes and seven researched outlines. Work toward one release daily once that buffer is sustainable. If research or visual accuracy takes longer, move a ready episode forward rather than filling a weak script with invented detail.

Prepare only the next 3–5 scripts in detail. Reorder later slots after the first seven releases using retention, shares, saves, meaningful comments and follows relative to reach. Compare patterns across several posts; one result does not establish a winning formula.

## National specials and their opening shots

**EP07 · పూరీ జగన్నాథుడు**  
Spoken opening: పూరీ జగన్నాథుడి రథం ఏ గుడికి వెళ్తుంది?  
Frame one: A wheel starts to turn under the title “పూరీ జగన్నాథుడు · ఒడిశా”. Pull wide to reveal three distinct chariots and the devotees pulling the ropes. In the next breath name the destination, గుండిచా ఆలయం. Follow Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra through the outward procession, why tradition associates Gundicha with the Lord’s birthplace, and the Bahuda return. Check the chariot designs against Odisha references before rendering. N1–N2.

**EP14 · తిరువనంతపురం పద్మనాభస్వామి**  
Spoken opening: పద్మనాభస్వామిని మూడు తలుపుల్లోంచి ఎందుకు చూస్తారు?  
Frame one: An illustrated view catches the reclining deity’s face through the first doorway. The title “తిరువనంతపురం · పద్మనాభస్వామి” is already visible. Move sideways so viewers understand that three openings reveal one figure. Then tell the Divakara Muni account in the Anantashayana tradition: the sage cannot take in the immense form, asks for a form three times the length of his staff, and the Lord grants it. Return to the doors for the payoff. Describe the middle sightline simply as the middle of the body. Detailed guide descriptions differ. Present the sage’s request as a temple tradition. N3–N4.

## Opening test for the series

On the first moving frame, typeset the temple and place in Telugu. Begin speaking the recognisable place or deity name on the first breath. Aim for a viewer to identify the temple and the puzzle by seconds 3–4. For longer names show the full name immediately and keep the delivery natural. Play the opening muted, then audio-only. Check both with a Telugu speaker seeing it for the first time. Judge the exported cut at phone size. Record whether the viewer can name both without prompting. After release, compare initial retention and available skip/stayed-to-watch data across the first batch. A difference between two temples can reflect topic or visuals as well as opening words.

The five revised spoken openings, covers and their matching cues are in the EP02–06 Production Book. EP01 keeps its approved cut. Source guidance for early attention: [YouTube creator conversation](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) and [Meta for Business short video on the first three seconds in ads](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OHX1ctQn6NY). The 3–4 second comprehension test is our editorial target.

## Episode 2 — Kolanupaka Someshwara

### Why this is worth making

An unfamiliar local story can create discovery, while a recognisable nearby shrine provides a regional connection. This is an editorial judgement; there is no audience-performance evidence for this episode yet. Its likely shrine/teacher setting should be easier to stage than a multi-location chase, subject to the final story.

### Supported starting point

Heritage Telangana records a literary tradition associating Renukacharya's emergence from, and eventual return into, the local self-manifested linga. It also describes the Someswara temple group and the site's sculptures and inscriptions. Tell the saint's emergence as a tradition, not a witnessed historical event. [K1]

The published epigraphic collection records 1067 grants connected with Svayambhu Somesvara at Kollipaka. This supports a historical worship/endowment context; it does not identify who first built the present temple. [K2, printed p.168, entries 147–148]

### Working Telugu opening

> కొలనుపాక సోమేశ్వర లింగం నుంచి ఓ గురువు వచ్చాడట. ఎవరాయన?

This is a working hook, not a locked narration. Check the local pronunciation and approved visual reference first.

### Story structure to research

1. Establish the actual Someshwara shrine and Kolanupaka location.
2. Introduce the attributed Renukacharya tradition and its source.
3. Tell the emergence and the teacher's role using the selected literary account. Do not invent pupils, dialogue, opponents, miracles or a journey to bridge gaps.
4. Complete the return-to-linga portion if confirmed in that same telling.
5. Connect the tradition with the shrine as worshipped today, using observed references.
6. End with a short historical anchor from the inscriptions if it helps the story. The opening must not become a list of dynasties and dates.

The Production Book now supplies the middle from the Siddhanta Shikhamani, chapters 4–5, and the return from chapter 21. Use its latest draft for production.

### Identity and accuracy checks

- This episode is about Someshwara/Someswara, not Kulpakji, the nearby Jain temple.
- The district's general 'Kolanupaka Temple' page mainly illustrates the Jain complex. Do not use those pictures as Someshwara architectural references.
- Do not borrow the Jain temple's Mahavira statue description for the Shiva linga.
- Do not infer a Moon/Daksha story merely from the name Someshwara. Establish its local relevance first.
- Do not repeat a round '800 years old' claim as a precise chronology. Inscriptions, later additions and the present fabric are different questions.
- Keep Renukacharya distinct from other similarly named figures and compare versions before selecting one.
- Reference the linga, saint's iconography, sanctum, dress and present-day worship separately.
- Do not generically mirror sacred attributes or right/left ritual gestures.

### Low-cost visual approach

Use a referenced map, a clean shrine plate, an accurate linga illustration, a separately designed teacher figure or key poses, light, lamps and particles. Stage characters and props on separate layers from the beginning. An animated light reveal may represent a legendary emergence, but label it as an artistic depiction rather than an accurate reconstruction.

Reserve a paid video shot only if an essential action cannot read clearly with designed poses or local animation. Do not build a general-purpose human rig before a small test demonstrates acceptable motion. Existing Mallanna artwork can inform palette and rendering, but is not an appropriate substitute for the saint or another deity.

No fixed clip quota or episode cost is set here. Quote actual image assets, clip duration, narration, any outsourced illustration and anticipated repair allowance after the source-led storyboard exists.

## Production rules for all 30 episodes

1. Research a claim table before writing narration. Keep exact source URLs, relevant passages/page numbers, tradition/version and contradictions.
2. Write a complete Telugu script. Use clear Telangana-flavoured speech without making an Andhra story sound caricatured. Preserve names and local terminology.
3. Approve the script revision and culturally specific reference pack together. Text changes invalidate the relevant approval.
4. Generate one selected narration take where the interface permits it, retrieve by the working History adapter and record its actual cost. Reuse the audio when adjusting pauses.
5. Derive the shot clock from the recording. For each beat identify what changes on screen and whether a gesture actually needs continuous motion.
6. Classify every asset as reuse, local code, new illustration/pose, or paid video. Count all new artwork needed for clean backgrounds and movable parts.
7. Quote total episode cost, not only the last batch of clips. Do not add percentages of savings before comparing finished episodes at acceptable quality.
8. Keep geometry-led explanations in the same illustrated visual language. A bare engineering-style diagram must not unexpectedly replace temple artwork.
9. Use paid video for necessary difficult action. No automatic paid retries or recurring production activation without the configured limits.
10. Check story continuity, deity identity, right/left hands, ritual geometry, captions against speech and actual phone readability. Pixel-change measurements alone cannot approve cultural or visual correctness.
11. Export a final MP4, cover, captions, source note, platform text and actual-cost record. Human viewing remains the last check before publication.

## Reusable library to build gradually

Maps and location labels; Telugu captions; dusk/day/night lighting; lamps; turmeric/kumkum particles; an illustrated inscription reveal; clean background staging; pose-cut timing; appropriate costume/prop references. Reuse generic technical components freely. Reuse culturally specific characters, objects or gestures only when appropriate for that temple and telling.
