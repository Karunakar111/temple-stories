## The retell sentence

> *"The turmeric people throw at Mallanna's temple? He stole it. Disguised as a gardener,
> for months. And a goddess lost her hand getting him home."*
>
> And the second one, for anyone who has stood next to a patnam:
> *"That big drawing on the ground isn't a rangoli. The priest is drawing the story while
> he sings it — and afterwards people take the powder home to their fields."*

## Why two parts, and where the cut is

Told whole at a storyteller's pace, this runs about two and a half minutes. Karunakar said
not to compress, so it's two episodes. The cut falls on the strongest beat in the story —
**the moment the con breaks: he has the bandari, and someone has seen.**

Each part keeps the series signature (object → story → object) on its own terms:
- **Part 1** opens on turmeric in *the viewer's* hand and ends on turmeric in *Mallanna's*
  hand, for the first time. Your hand / his hand.
- **Part 2** opens on his hand and ends on yours. The loop closes across both.

## Decisions baked in

- **The chant opens and closes every episode: "కొమురవెల్లి మల్లన్నకు — కోటి దండాలు!"**
  This is what devotees actually cry at this temple — *"మల్లన్న స్వామి, మమ్మేలు స్వామి,
  కొమురవెల్లి మల్లన్న కోటి దండాలు"* — and Telugu papers use *కోటి దండాలు* in their own jatara
  headlines. Chosen over a generic *జై మల్లన్న* for three reasons: it is specific to this god
  rather than pan-Indian; **it contains the temple's name**, so the audio hook and the
  identity land in the same breath; and devotees already type it, so closing on it asks for
  a comment they were going to leave anyway.
- **Location is spoken, not only shown** — "ఎనభై ఐదు కిలోమీటర్లు… సిద్దిపేట జిల్లా" — because
  most people hear a reel before they read it. The pin card carries it for sound-off viewers.

- **Destination unnamed** (Karunakar, 2026-11-22). He goes "far away." The ones who chase
  him are "the guards." Every dramatic beat survives; nothing is invented or contradicted.
- **One card per part**, at the hinge. Part 1: the condition. Part 2: the payoff.
- **"Why here?"** answered in Part 2: *he came back to this very hill.*
- **Frame line is now "ఇక్కడ ఒగ్గు పూజారులు పాడే కథ ప్రకారం"** — more accurate than
  *sthala puranam* (this story is an Oggu Katha, not a written text), and it plants the word
  so Part 2's payoff lands on something the viewer has already heard. If Karunakar prefers
  the familiarity of *స్థలపురాణం ప్రకారం*, it's a one-phrase swap.
- **Oggu Katha and the patnam live in Part 2's payoff, not Part 1's setup.** Said early
  they're exposition; said at the end they're the reveal — *the story you just watched is
  still being sung and drawn, here, in this turmeric.*

## Notes for the Telugu read

Written as spoken storytelling, not prose. Short sentences. The pauses are in the dashes.

**Telangana dialect — decided yes, and applied.** The narration above is Telangana Telugu,
not standard: `-ిండు / -ిండ్రు` past tense (బయల్దేరిండు, చేసిండు, ఆగిండు, పెట్టిండ్రు, చూసిండ్రు),
Telangana locatives (చేతిల, ఊళ్ల, జాతరల, క్షణంల, పుస్తకంల), and the regional words that
actually carry the accent — అడివి, ఇగ, ఎట్ల, పోలే, వదల్లే, పాడుకుంటనే, చెప్పుండ్రి, కాపాడనీకి.

**ఆగిండు kept over ఓపిక పట్టిండు.** One verb carrying the whole con is stronger than three
words explaining it — and the line before it has already done the explaining.

**⚠ This dialect pass is mine, and I am not a native Telangana speaker.** It follows the
documented forms, but rhythm is not grammar. **Karunakar should listen to the generated
narration before the asset build begins** — that is the cheap moment to catch a line that
is correct on paper and wrong in the mouth. Flagging two I'd check first:
- P1 §6 `తోటమాలిగ చేరిండు` — does a god "joining as a gardener" want చేరిండు or కుదిరిండు?
- P2 §2 `కాపలవాళ్లు వెంటబడ్డరు` — వెంటబడ్డరు vs వెంటపడ్డరు; I went with the voiced form.
