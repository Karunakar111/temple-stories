## The one thing a viewer should remember

> He didn't stand above the communities who called him. He married into them — and that
> is why his temple still has two sets of priests.

## Register

Told as **sthala puranam**, per Karunakar. Open the door once —
**"ఇక్కడి స్థలపురాణం ప్రకారం…"** — then tell it with full confidence and full colour.
No hedging inside the frame. No dates, no funding, no administration: nobody cares.

## Why it's built this way

- **Shot 1 is recognition, not information.** The viewer has physically stood next to a
  patnam. Start where they already are, then tell them it wasn't what they thought.
- **Shot 3 is the whole episode's image.** A god made of termite-mound earth, rising for
  frightened herders. If one asset gets the most generations, it is IMG-04.
- **Shot 7 must be symmetrical or the episode lies.** The narration says he married into
  both communities as equals. If the composition favours one woman, the composition wins.
  The dissolve from the two wives to the two priests is the single best idea here — it
  makes a 500-year-old marriage explain something you can go and see on Sunday.
- **The ending turns my research gap into the hook.** I genuinely could not find what
  Medalamma's brothers demanded — tellings differ. Rather than invent it or skip it,
  the episode *asks*. Honest, and it is the strongest possible comment prompt: a question
  only the audience can answer, which sets up Part 2 and feeds the archive.
- **No call to action beyond that one question.** No like-and-subscribe over the last image.
