## Why we are here

The procedural puppet reads as mechanical: exposed joints, rectangular limbs, fixed
eyes, a rigid frontal pose. All four are symptoms of the same cause — the figure was
*assembled from parts to be animated* rather than *designed and then broken into
parts*. These briefs reverse that order.

# Direction A — "Temple Icon"

**The god as you meet him at the shrine.** Frontal, close, devotional. The viewer is
standing in front of him, not watching a story.

- **Proportions:** squat and powerful — **4.5 head-heights**, broad shoulders, heavy
  chest, short thick limbs. Deliberately closer to the proportions of the clay murti
  than to a human body.
- **Eyes:** very large, round, wide open, looking straight out at the viewer.
- **Pose:** near-symmetrical, one arm raised in the throw, the other open at his side.
  Frontality is a *choice* here, not a limitation — it is how an icon is composed.
- **Palette — tight, 6 colours:** deep murti blue `#1E4FA3`, bright green `#1F8A4C`,
  magenta `#C2246B`, marigold `#F0A32A`, rich gold `#F0B323`, near-black ground `#140E14`.
- **Surroundings:** the dark rock recess immediately behind him, two oil lamps, hanging
  marigold strings at the top of frame, worn stone underfoot. Shallow, enclosed, no sky.
- **Light:** lamp-light from below and in front — the way a murti is actually lit.

**Risk:** static. If the throw does not read, this becomes a portrait and not a story.

# Direction B — "Folk Narrative"

**The register of the Jagannath reference.** A story being told, with a world around
it. The viewer is watching, not worshipping.

- **Proportions:** stylised but humane — **6 head-heights**, longer limbs, visible
  weight shift. Capable of gesture and movement.
- **Eyes:** almond, in three-quarter view, with a visible lash line — expressive
  rather than fixed. He looks at what he is doing.
- **Pose:** three-quarter, weight on the back foot, torso twisted into the throw.
  Asymmetry is the point.
- **Palette — warm and controlled, 7 colours:** amber `#C2702A`, dusk violet `#5A4A72`,
  night indigo `#2B2546`, marigold `#F0A32A`, deep blue skin `#2E63B8`,
  bone `#F2E4CE`, ink `#2A1B14`.
- **Surroundings:** the hill at dusk, the shrine small behind him in its rock, the
  Deccan opening out, a few pilgrims climbing far below. Scale and distance.
- **Light:** low sun from behind, rim-lighting the figure and the turmeric.

**Risk:** this is the reference's look. Executed loosely it will read as derivative.

# Direction C — "Cheriyal scroll"

**The region's own narrative painting form.** Cheriyal scroll painting is Telangana's
traditional storytelling art, a local development of **Nakashi** work, now made only
in **Cheriyal town, Siddipet district**.

**Corrections, 2026-11-23:**
- **GI year is disputed.** Sources give 2007, 2008 (registration no. 190), and 2010.
  **Do not state a year on screen** without checking the GI registry directly. Safe
  wording: "GI-protected."
- **Komuravelli is not in Cheriyal mandal.** It has its own **Komuravelli mandal**
  within Siddipet district. Under the pre-2016 districting it did sit in Cheriyal
  mandal of the old Warangal district, which is where the earlier claim came from.
  The accurate statement is: **Komuravelli and Cheriyal are neighbouring mandals in
  the same district.** Still a real and close connection — just not the same mandal.

The form is built for exactly what we are doing: a vertical scroll of horizontal
panels, unrolled by a bard as he narrates, one panel shown per beat of the story.

**[A] Verified characteristics:**
- **Red predominates** as the background
- Vivid, mostly **primary** colours
- **Proportion is set by narrative importance** — the most important figure is the
  largest and the most detailed. This directly solves the "rigid proportions" problem:
  Mallanna is big because he matters, not because of anatomy.
- **Floral borders separate horizontal panels**
- Vertical format, horizontal panels
- Painted by the **Nakashi** artist community

**[?] Do NOT claim:** that Cheriyal scrolls were made for Oggu Katha, Golla or Kuruma
performers. The documented client communities are the barber, toddy-tapper, washerman,
leatherworker, fisherman, weaver and farmer castes, via the Kaki Padagollu balladeers.
Golla and Kuruma are **not** among them. The honest framing is "the narrative painting
tradition of this district," not "the painting tradition of this story."

- **Proportions:** hierarchical. Mallanna occupies most of the panel height; everything
  else is small. Round face, heavy jaw, large eyes, decorative rather than anatomical.
- **Palette — Cheriyal's own, 6 colours:** red ground `#C0392B`, white `#F5EFE0`,
  yellow `#F2C230`, green `#2E7D4F`, blue `#2E5FA3`, black outline `#1A1310`.
- **Surroundings:** a stylised floral border framing the panel; a simple flat band of
  ground; the shrine as a flat decorative motif, not perspective. Space is symbolic.

**Risk:** the least like the Jagannath reference Karunakar approved. It is also the
most distinctive and the most defensible culturally.

### Character, separated for animation
Each part needs **clean alpha and a marked pivot point**, drawn slightly oversized at
the joint so rotation never opens a gap — the defect that made the procedural version
look mechanical.

| Layer | Pivot |
|---|---|
| `head` | base of neck |
| `crown` | separate from head, so it can be drawn once and reused |
| `torso` | hips |
| `arm-L-upper`, `arm-L-fore`, `hand-L` | shoulder / elbow / wrist |
| `arm-R-upper`, `arm-R-fore` | shoulder / elbow |
| `hand-R-closed`, `hand-R-open` | wrist — **two states, so the throw never swaps the hand away** |
| `leg-L-upper`, `leg-L-lower`, `foot-L` | hip / knee / ankle |
| `leg-R-upper`, `leg-R-lower`, `foot-R` | hip / knee / ankle |
| `garland-front` | drawn separately so it can lag behind the torso |
| `dhoti-overlay` | separate from the legs, so cloth can swing |
